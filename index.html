IoT Roadside-to-vehicle speed regulation, Cameroon

The road tells the bus how fast to go.

Roadside stations along the Douala, Yaoundé and Bafoussam corridors send each road section's speed limit to the vehicle. Over the limit, the driver is warned for two seconds, then the speed comes down gently. No sudden braking.

01 The problem

Too many buses drive above the limit on Cameroon's high-risk roads. This system brings every one of them back to the limit, automatically.

On the corridors linking Douala, Yaoundé, Edéa, Mbanga, Nkongsamba, Dschang and Bafoussam, speed limits change from one road section to the next, and drivers do not always follow them.

Road speed regulation connects the road to the vehicle. Roadside stations send the limit of their section, the vehicle compares its own speed, warns the driver, and slows the vehicle down gradually when needed. Police see every violation in their sector as it happens.

02 The system

The three parts of the system

Each part has one job. Together they form a closed loop from the road to the vehicle and back to the police.

Part 01

The roadside station

A solar-powered unit beside the road, one per section. It broadcasts the speed limit set by the local police station to every vehicle passing through.

  • Detects each vehicle entering its coverage zone
  • Sends the limit of its road segment, set remotely by police or the administration
  • Can change at any time: rain, road works, accident
  • Also relays police messages to drivers
Part 02

The vehicle unit

A small device in each bus. It receives the limit, measures the speed by GPS, warns the driver and, if the agency is set to it, brings the speed down gradually to the limit.

  • Warning for 2 seconds before any action
  • Gentle reduction, never sudden braking
  • Every warning and reduction is reported
Part 03

The control centre

A server collects the events sent by every vehicle. Police stations and travel agencies follow them on their own screens and act on them.

  • Live list of vehicles in each sector
  • Violations sorted by severity, with alerts for high-risk situations
  • History of speeds and violations for each road segment
  • Signals to one driver, one agency or everyone
03 Regulation

How regulation works

What happens when a bus enters a 50 km/h section too fast.

  1. 01The station broadcastsThe roadside station sends the speed limit of its road section to every vehicle passing.
  2. 02The vehicle comparesThe vehicle unit checks its own speed against that limit several times a second.
  3. 03The driver is warnedOver the limit, a warning sounds and flashes for two seconds.
  4. 04Speed comes down gentlyThe speed is reduced step by step to the limit, and the event is reported to the police.

05 Who uses it

One app, three users

Each person signs in with their own code. A code from one role never works in another.

Drivers

  • Speedometer that turns red over the limit
  • The limit of the current section
  • Spoken and vibrating warnings
  • Messages from the police

Police stations

  • Set the limit of each road segment in their town
  • Warn only, or warn then reduce, per agency
  • See live vehicles, violations and alerts
  • Search the history of each segment
  • Send messages to drivers

Administrators

  • Set the speed limit of every road segment
  • Create driver, police and agency accounts
  • Codes sent to each person by email
  • Each agency manages only its own drivers
  • Reset forgotten passwords

The corridor

    07 Technology

    Built on open, proven technology

    • IoT messaging with MQTT. Vehicles publish every event as JSON on the topic corridor/speedevents. Limits and police messages go back to the road the same way.
    • Phone GPS or a dedicated unit. Speed comes from the vehicle's GPS, so the system works on any bus.
    • Python server with a database. Flask and SQLite keep accounts, limits and the history of every event.
    • Secure accounts. Passwords are stored hashed, repeated wrong attempts are blocked, and the app is served over HTTPS.
